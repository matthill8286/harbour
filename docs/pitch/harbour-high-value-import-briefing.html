The mess

When the car is worth a house, WhatsApp is a bad tracking system.

This is not a supermarket hatchback. The owner already paid a fortune, or cares about this car like it is one. They will ask where it is. Today the answer lives in a spreadsheet, a chat, and a GPS login they are not allowed to see.

What goes wrong

  • A six-figure car, and nobody can show a clean trail
  • The owner gets a photo from a port, then silence for two weeks
  • Staff mix “what it cost us” with “what we told the customer”
  • The tracker moves onto the next job and the old history vanishes

Why this car, not every car

  • High-value and special cars make people anxious. That is the job
  • High-value, rare, or just loved — few jobs, a lot riding on each one
  • The desk is trusted with someone else’s pride and joy
  • A cheap fleet van does not need this. A car someone is precious about does
Who uses it: the people running the import. Who watches: the owner, on a simple page that never shows costs, suppliers, or the number on the tracker box.
